# How to open the room

**Status:** settled
**Date:** 16 August 2026
**Context:** written for the family conversation of 17 August, but it holds for any moment where a lot of finished work meets people who were not holding the pen.

## The problem

The volume of work is itself the problem. Everything built so far is good, and the more polished it looks, the louder it says *decided*. Even where people were consulted, output at scale changes what consultation feels like.

So showing less is not a presentation tactic. It is the whole thing.

## The move

Explain, do not apologise. There is nothing to apologise for: everything done so far has been good and has benefited the family. But naming how it might *feel* is not the same as conceding a charge, and it is simply good hosting.

## The words

> "One thing before I start. I've built a lot of this out. Partly that's had to happen, and honestly, partly it's just me. When I'm excited about something I run at it. That's how I am, and I don't think that's a bad thing.
>
> But I know I don't know everything, and I'm not right about everything, even when I've got strong views. And when someone shows you a lot of finished-looking stuff, I know it can land as 'here's the thing' rather than 'what do you reckon'.
>
> What actually matters to me is what we could build together. So I'd rather show you less today and hear more."

## Why it works

The pace has a reason, and the reason is passion, which is true and likeable. Nobody argues with someone for caring too much.

The load-bearing sentence is *I'm not right about everything, even when I've got strong views*, because it is the one that actively invites disagreement. Anyone holding a view they have not said yet needs that permission.

And *partly that's had to happen* stops the passion explanation from being the only reason. Someone did have to build it.

## The commitment it creates

Having said "I'd rather hear more", the recap must genuinely be short and the silence after the observations must be real. Saying it and then talking for twenty five minutes does more damage than never saying it.

## The matching close

If the opening softens, the ask softens with it. Not a proposal with a shape already decided, but an invitation with the shape still open:

> "So what I'm really asking is, would there be an appetite for us to do the next one together? And if there would be, what would that even look like? Because I genuinely don't know, and I'd rather work it out with you than turn up with it."

The last clause is the whole meeting in one line. It offers people the thing that matters most, which is shaping it rather than joining it.

## The general principle

Ask first, and ask by name, whoever is most likely to feel they were not consulted. Being asked first signals status in a way nothing else does.
