# Growth and evolution

**Status:** settled
**Date:** 16 August 2026

## The shape

Everything expanded except the point.

| | CQ1 | CQ2 | CQ3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made | a quiz, to a brief | games, videos, slides, music | whatever they choose |
| Media | one | four, unplanned | open |
| Format | virtual, two weeks | one room, one day | continuous, with a day in it |
| Delivered by | Uncle B | Uncle B, with a platform behind it | all of us, live and recorded |
| Family role | audience | attendees | co-deliverers |
| Seen by | the family | the family | outside the room |
| AI was | the subject | the tool | the route |

## Three layers of growth, all evidenced

**The kids.** Hannah asked what a database was in March and had one running behind a live multiplayer quiz two weeks later. Ezekiel solved the GitHub token problem himself in April and was named best team leader in August. Emma had an honorary badge at four and built her own game at six. Elsie said in April that you need confidence and to present clearly, and in August won most creative for the explanation that made the whole thing make sense.

**The families.** Watching, then attending, then delivering. That is the arc, and it makes the Code Quest 3 ask the next step rather than a jump.

**What is possible.** In March the room had to be taught what a prompt was. By August there was a platform with an automated pipeline putting a child's game on the real internet at its own address.

## The line

AI went from the subject being taught, to the tool they used, to something they barely mention. Tool, route, invisible.

The honest version of "it was never about code" is therefore sharper: it was never even about AI. AI was the route to *I want to create*.

## The argument this produces

Four of the six kids kept building after August with nothing helping them. No app, no prompts, no structure, nobody checking in.

**What happens if there is something there to help them?**

Not a projection. An observation with an obvious question attached, which the room can answer for itself.

## Why the events keep getting bigger

Between Code Quest 1 and 2, the only thing that carried forward was what the kids kept in their own heads: their skills and their confidence. That alone doubled the ambition of the event.

The loop, once the app exists:

**The event makes the memories → the app holds them → the app keeps them building → what they build gets shown at the next event → the event gets bigger.**

Code Quest 1 and 2 ran that loop with the middle two steps missing. Code Quest 3 is the first with all four.

## Why the app exists

Someone will ask. The wrong answer is anything about platforms, architecture or subscriptions.

The right answer is: **so it doesn't stop.**

A connected experience, online and offline. The event is the peak. The app is what carries it between peaks, in both directions: the films, badges and archive from the day live in the app, and the work done in the app gets celebrated on the next day.

## Consequence for delivery

Delivery is not only in person. Recorded content, training and explainers on the platform are delivery, and they outlive the event they were made for.

This matters practically. Co-delivering does not require everyone to be free on a Saturday, which is the single biggest barrier for people with jobs and young children. Fifteen minutes recorded on presenting, or storytelling, or how money works, is real delivery and it becomes part of the library.

It also resolves a tension: if every adult is delivering on the day, nobody is in the audience, and the parents watching was the emotional payload of Code Quest 2. Asynchronous contribution means some can deliver and some can watch.
