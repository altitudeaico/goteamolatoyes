# It was never really about code

**Status:** settled
**Date:** 16 August 2026

## The move

Do not assert the reframe. Diagnose it, in Mayowa's three-part structure from the commercial identity work:

**What we thought it was.** A coding thing for the kids. The clue is in the name.

**What we found.** Grandma judged the whole of Code Quest 2 and gave out seven awards. Not one of them was technical. Best presenter, best team leader, most creative, for overcoming, calm under pressure, perseverance, participation. Nobody briefed her. That is just what she saw. And in April, asked what she had learned in a fortnight of building, Elsie did not say databases or GitHub. She said you need confidence and to present clearly. She was nine.

**What we resolve.** We built a coding thing and accidentally built something else, and the something else is the part that matters.

## Why this shape

Assertion invites a rebuttal, because we genuinely did teach them to code: prompting, databases, GitHub, hosting. Someone can fairly say "well, you called it Code Quest." Diagnosis does not have that hole, because it starts by conceding the obvious thing and then shows the evidence pointing elsewhere.

Two independent witnesses reached the same diagnosis from completely different evidence. Mayowa looked at the architecture and found the unusual investment was not in curriculum but in making a child's work safe enough to publish. Grandma looked at the children and gave out seven non-technical awards. Neither knew what the other was doing.

## The rules have changed

Keep this concrete or it drifts. The specific version: the gap between a child having an idea and having a real thing other people can use has collapsed. Not a prediction. It is on the record, with a URL, built in an afternoon by a nine year old.

## Lines worth using

From Mayowa, better than anything written since:

- "Coding is the first medium. Building is the identity."
- "The desired reaction is not 'I learned to code.' It is 'I made something, and it exists.'"
- "Children should not grow up believing technology is something other people build for them."

## Lines to avoid in a family setting

Commercial identity. Source of advantage. Category map. Disproportionate value. Publishing platform for young digital builders. All correct, all consultancy register, all guaranteed to make it sound like a presentation rather than a conversation.

## How to land it

End on a question, not a conclusion. "Did you see that too, or is it just me?" If someone answers with something you had not noticed, the point has landed harder than any statement could.
