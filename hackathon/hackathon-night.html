Four Apps in Two Weeks

Two teams. Four fully working apps — live on the internet, real databases, real multiplayer. Before this bootcamp most of these kids didn't know what a database was. Tonight they demoed one to the whole family.

👑

Elsie on Confidence

When the kids shared what they learned, Elsie went beyond the technical. She talked about the importance of confidence and clear presentation. Not a coding lesson — a life lesson from an 11 year old.

"You need confidence and to present clearly." — Elsie
🧠

They Actually Got It

The kids explained what they learned in their own words — clear prompts, what a database does, how to deploy to GitHub. Two weeks ago they couldn't define these things. Tonight they taught them to their parents.

🐛

The Bug on Demo Night

Hannah and Sophia's Cousin Quiz froze live — questions wouldn't advance. Gutting timing. But that's exactly what happens in real software. Every developer has had a demo go wrong. They're in very good company.

"Even on demo night, bugs happen. That's real software." — The lesson

In their own words

What They Learned 🧠

On the night each kid answered: "What's one thing you know now that you didn't know two weeks ago?" Here's what came back.

💬

How to talk to AI clearly

The most important skill of the whole bootcamp. Not code — words. Clear, specific, detailed prompts get results. Vague prompts get vague results. Every kid learned this the hard way at least once.

🗄️

What a database actually is

Two weeks ago Hannah asked "what is a database?" Tonight she has one running behind a live multiplayer quiz. That's the journey in one sentence.

🌐

How to put something on the internet

GitHub Pages, repositories, pushing code, live URLs — they understand the difference between something that lives on your screen and something that lives on the internet. That's a real and rare skill.

👑

Confidence and clear presentation

Elsie said it — it's not enough to build something good. You have to show it and talk about it with confidence. That lesson goes way beyond coding. That lesson lasts a lifetime.
