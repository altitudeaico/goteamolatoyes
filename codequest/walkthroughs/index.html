What the demo shows

  • 0:00The public site. "Kids build real things. The world gets to play them." A child builds a game, a grown-up checks it over, minutes later it is live at its own address, something they can hand to a grandparent, a cousin or a classroom.
  • 0:30The kid app, empty. "Nothing here yet, let's make something!" Two ways in: give me a game to start with, or see what other kids made. Then the upload panel: choose a folder, or a zip.
  • 1:00The parent view. The family, each child with a public display name rather than their real one, QR sign in, reset PIN.
  • 1:30Status, in the child's own view: pending, rejected with the parent's note written in plain words, and live with a play link.
  • 2:00The published thing, running at its own address.

Worth noticing, against the earlier review

Three of the four gaps raised in the MVP review are already closed in this recording, which the written status report did not make obvious.

  • ✓Empty states exist and are good. "Nothing here yet, let's make something" with two next actions, rather than a bare empty list.
  • ✓Rejection reads like a person wrote it. The parent's note appears in the child's own view with a send it again button. That is the most educational moment in the loop and it is built.
  • ✓Status is legible. Pending, rejected and live are distinct and a child can tell them apart at a glance.
  • —Still open: referral attribution, the child's own page as a single shareable surface, and play counts. Same three as before.

The copy is also notably strong. "It needs an index.html inside" and "you can also drag it here" are the sort of lines that only come from watching someone actually get stuck.
