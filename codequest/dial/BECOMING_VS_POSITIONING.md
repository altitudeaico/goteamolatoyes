# Becoming
## How far is it from Mayowa's positioning?

Short answer: **not far at all on identity and agency, and directly opposed on one thing only.** That one thing is whether the becoming is fictional or real. Get that right and the two are the same idea. Get it wrong and they cancel each other out.

---

## Where they already agree, more than it looks

Mayowa's document is not actually a document about coding versus creating. Read again, it is a document about **identity**.

- "TEKTON comes from Greek tekton: builder, craftsperson, maker. **It names the child's identity rather than the medium.**"
- "Children should not grow up believing technology is something other people build for them. **They should grow up knowing the world is theirs to build.**"
- The product test he proposes: "**does this give the child more agency** to make something of their own?"
- "Publication should feel **ceremonial and real**."

That last line matters most. He is not asking for a flat, unemotional utility. He explicitly wants ceremony. A company belief phrased as "should grow up knowing" is itself a statement about becoming, over time, through experience.

And your three components map almost exactly onto his loop:

| Your phrasing | His model |
|---|---|
| a quest they take on | CONSUME → MAKE |
| empowered with things that unleash new creativity | agency, and AI that expands rather than replaces the author |
| build whatever they imagine | MAKE |
| and share it with the world | PUBLISH → BE SEEN → MAKE AGAIN |

So the shape of the journey is not in dispute.

---

## The one real conflict

He says, plainly: **"Status, points and gamification should not overwhelm authorship"** and **"no coding clichés, mascots, neon terminals or default gamification."**

A quest with ranks and an Awakening is, on its face, exactly that. So the question is not whether becoming belongs. It is:

> **Does the child become something inside our fiction, or something true outside it?**

**Fictional becoming.** You reach Level 3 and we call you a Mentor. The title exists only in our product. If we shut down tomorrow, it evaporates. This is what he is warning against, and he is right to. It also fails the Elsie test badly, because an eleven year old who wants to be a beauty creator does not want to be a Quester.

**Real becoming.** You actually become a person who has made things that strangers use. The evidence is external, checkable, and yours. If we shut down tomorrow, you are still someone who shipped four things and taught two people. That is not gamification. That is a track record.

Your own martial arts instinct already had this right. A black belt is not points. It is a verified capability that the world outside the dojo respects. The ceremony is real because the thing behind it is real.

---

## The synthesis

**Attach the ceremony to the real threshold, and put the fiction in the film, not the interface.**

Three rules make becoming survive Mayowa's critique intact:

**1. Every transformation marks a real, external fact.**
Not a points total. The first Awakening fires the moment the first thing goes live at its own address, because that genuinely is the day the child changed from someone who uses the internet to someone who is on it. He asked for publication to feel ceremonial. This is how.

**2. What is unlocked is capability, not decoration.**
"Empowered with things that unleash new levels of creativity" is the strongest part of your framing and it is fully compatible with him, on one condition: what unlocks must be real power, not cosmetics. Sound. Saving. Multiplayer. A second medium. Your own space. Better AI tools. He allows AI explicitly, provided "it should not become the author by default." A ladder of real capability is not gamification, it is an apprenticeship.

**3. The world lives in the events and the films. The product stays plain.**
This is the conclusion we already reached when the invented vocabulary came out of the app. It holds here. The Awakening film at the event is spectacular and mythic. The interface says "your game is live." Kids get the myth in the room, on the day, in the film, and the app never makes anyone learn a private language to use it.

---

## The blank

You wrote "become the [fill in blank]" and I think the blank is the whole answer.

**The child fills it in. Not us.**

Elsie did this without being asked. She did not become a Quester. She became **Butterflii Studio**, a name she chose, for a studio she invented, which she then fronted to camera unprompted. Theodore became the person who makes flight games. That is the becoming, and it is self-authored.

So the promise is not "become a coder" and not "become a Mogul". It is closer to:

> **Become the thing you already wanted to be. We give you the power and the place to prove it.**

That resolves the Elsie test completely. The girl who wants to be a beauty creator does not have to become a coder to belong here. She becomes a person with her own studio, her own name, her own thing on the internet, and code was just what she used. She is not converted into our world. She is equipped for hers.

It also resolves the naming argument underneath all of this. A name built on the medium, like CodeQuest, describes what she used. A name built on authorship, like BuiltBy, describes what she did.

## What this changes concretely

| Currently | Under the synthesis |
|---|---|
| Levels named Quester, Builder, Mentor, Mogul | Named for real states: made something, published, people use it, others learn from you. The child's own studio name is the identity that matters |
| The Awakening as a mysterious sealed reward | The Awakening fires at first publication, because that is the real threshold, and the film is made about that day |
| Progress as stars toward a rank | Progress as capability unlocking: sound, saving, a second medium, your own space, sharper tools |
| The quest world inside the interface | The quest world in the event, the film and the ceremony. The interface stays plain |
| We name what the child becomes | The child names it. The product's job is to make that name real and put it somewhere |

---

## The honest risks

**Becoming without a fiction can go flat.** The quest scaffolding is doing real motivational work, especially for the youngest kids, and stripping it entirely could leave something worthy and cold. The mitigation is that the ceremony moves rather than disappears: the event day, the transformation film and the moment of going live carry the emotion instead.

**"Become whatever you want" is vague at the point of sale.** A parent still buys a specific thing. Same resolution as before: sell the specific, deliver the broad. "Your child builds a real app and publishes it in a day" is the promise. The becoming is what they discover afterwards.

**Self-authored identity needs a floor.** A six year old cannot invent a studio name unaided, and should not be left staring at a blank box. There needs to be a scaffold: pick from some, then make it yours.

---

## The one-line test

If you are ever unsure whether a becoming mechanic belongs, ask:

> **If the company vanished tomorrow, would the child still be it?**

Published four things people play: yes. Taught someone younger to publish theirs: yes. Runs a studio with a name she chose: yes.

Level 3 Mentor with 340 stars: no.

Keep everything that survives the test. Cut everything that does not.
