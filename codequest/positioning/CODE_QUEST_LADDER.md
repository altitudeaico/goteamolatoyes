# The Code Quest Ladder
## A development pathway from first game to founder

Working draft. The curriculum spine for the Quester to Mogul journey. Written to be worked into the app, the events, the lessons feed and the mentor programme.

---

## First principles

1. **Levels mark what you can do, not what you know.** Every milestone is observable: a thing shipped, a talk given, a kid helped. There are no tests. The publish pipeline, the clips feed and the event Tellings are the assessment.
2. **Four strands run through every level.** What changes as you climb is the ratio, not the menu. A Quester is mostly Build. A Mogul is mostly Voice and People. That shift in ratio is the founder shape.
3. **Teaching is the soft skills curriculum.** Presenting, empathy, conflict resolution and leadership are not taught in the abstract. They are what mentoring and the Telling require, practised live.
4. **Every milestone is filmable.** Each one produces a moment for the clips feed, which feeds the content flywheel, which fills the next cohort.
5. **Stars are milestones.** The app already shows stars. A star equals one completed milestone. Fill the stars, pass the gate, level up. Gates are single sentences a parent and a mentor can verify together.

---

## The four strands

**BUILD** covers tech, coding and product: making things, understanding them, improving them, and eventually deciding what to build and what to cut.

**VOICE** covers marketing, messaging, storytelling and public speaking: naming things, explaining things, presenting to a room, and eventually launching to the world.

**PEOPLE** covers emotional intelligence, teamwork, collaboration, conflict resolution and leadership: from receiving and giving kindness to running a team and growing other people.

**INNER GAME** covers grit, resilience and the champion mindset: finishing what you start, surviving the thing breaking, coming back after the excitement wears off, and eventually taking losses publicly and sharing wins with the team.

## LEVEL 1 · QUESTER
### "I can make things"
Strand ratio: roughly 70% Build, 15% Voice, 10% People, 5% Inner Game.
Where it happens: the Code Quest day, plus the first fortnight at home.

**Build milestones**
- Ship your first game on the day. Starter template plus at least three changes that are yours.
- Understand the one file. Point at the code and say what three lines do.
- Use the AI helper to make one deliberate change and check it did what you asked.

**Voice milestones**
- The Telling: stand up for about 90 seconds. What it is, how to play it, one thing that went wrong.
- Name your creation. A real name, chosen by you.

**People milestones**
- Give one kind, specific compliment about another kid's build. Specific means naming the thing, not just saying it is good.
- Ask for help once. Asking is a skill and it gets a star.

**Inner Game milestones**
- Finish something in one day.
- The moment it broke and you kept going. A mentor logs it when they see it.

**GATE to Level 2:** Your first game is live at a link, you presented it, and you shared the link with at least one person outside the room.

---

## LEVEL 2 · BUILDER
### "I can make things people want"
Strand ratio: roughly 50% Build, 25% Voice, 15% People, 10% Inner Game.
Where it happens: at home over a cohort, guided by the lessons feed and the second build quest.

**Build milestones**
- Ship three builds from home. Each one adds a mechanic the last one did not have: a score, levels, saving, sound.
- Remix one other kid's build, with credit given on your page.
- Debug out loud once: say the three steps as you do them. Make it happen again, find where it lives, fix it.

**Voice milestones**
- Write the blurb on your own game page. That is product messaging and it is yours.
- Make one 30 second video about a build for the feed.
- Playtest with two real players. Watch them without helping. Change one thing because of what you saw. Watching players is where product sense starts.

**People milestones**
- One pair build: divide the work, then make the pieces fit together.
- Give structured feedback to another builder using I like, I wish, what if.

**Inner Game milestones**
- Beat the second build slump. Finish a build after the excitement wears off. This is the single most predictive milestone on the ladder and mentors treat it that way.
- Keep one broken thing on your page or in your notes, named honestly. Failure is part of the record.

**GATE to Level 3:** Three builds shipped, one of them playtested and changed, and you presented a before and after: what the players did, and what you changed because of it.

---

## LEVEL 3 · MENTOR
### "I can make others better"
Strand ratio: roughly 30% Build, 25% Voice, 35% People, 10% Inner Game.
Where it happens: serving at a Code Quest day and across a cohort as a junior mentor.

**Build milestones**
- Help debug someone else's build without touching their keyboard. Questions first: what did you expect, what happened instead, what did you change last.
- Ship one collaborative build in a shared team space.
- Make one scope cut on record: name what you dropped so the thing could ship, and why.

**Voice milestones**
- Teach one five minute lesson to younger kids, live or as a feed lesson.
- Compère or introduce a segment of a Code Quest day.
- Take questions after a Telling and answer on your feet.

**People milestones**
- Junior mentor a table of five or six kids for a session or a cohort.
- Mediate one creative disagreement using the house protocol: each person says what they want, then together find the third option neither had alone.
- Spot a frustrated kid before they give up and step in kindly. A senior mentor logs it when they see it.

**Inner Game milestones**
- Coach someone through a failure without fixing it for them. Resilience, transferred.
- Set a public goal for your table or team and own the result out loud, either way.

**GATE to Level 4:** You served a cohort as a mentor, you taught at least one lesson, and at least one kid you mentored shipped their game.

---

## LEVEL 4 · MOGUL
### "I can make things happen"
Strand ratio: roughly 20% Build, 30% Voice, 35% People, 15% Inner Game.
Where it happens: across the platform and at events, running like a founder in miniature.

**Build and product milestones**
- Own a franchise: one build with versions, a change log and a what is next list.
- Make one real trade off call with the reasons written down where your team can see them.

**Voice and marketing milestones**
- Run a launch: a trailer clip, fresh page copy, and a share plan.
- Read your numbers: plays, shares and referrals week on week, and say what you will try next because of them.
- Tell the story to adults: present at an event to parents, partners or judges.

**People and leadership milestones**
- Lead a small team of builders on one project: divide the work, keep everyone credited, get it shipped.
- Recruit and induct one brand new kid into their first cohort. The growth loop, personified.

**Inner Game milestones**
- The champion standard: set the bar for your team, take a loss publicly without blaming, and hand credit for wins to the people who did the work.
- The stewardship conversation: when earning privileges open, sit with your parent and agree what the money is for. Kids keep 100%. Moguls learn what that means.

**GATE: The Awakening.** Granted on the whole record of what you created, shipped, presented and who you helped. Verified by a mentor and your parent together. Never bought. Never rushed.

## How it wires into what already exists

- **The app.** Stars on the home screen are milestone counts. The Levels screen shows the four rungs. Lessons in the feed carry Counts toward Level 2 labels; every lesson maps to a named milestone.
- **The events.** The Telling is the Voice assessment at every level. Mentor and Mogul milestones are performed at events: compering, table mentoring, presenting to adults.
- **The lessons feed.** Each Builder milestone gets a 60 second lesson series. Watching is not the milestone; the shipped thing it leads to is.
- **The 2026 evidence.** The first event already assessed this ladder without naming it. Four of ten marks were for explaining, which is the Voice strand. The judge's awards mapped onto the strands: best presenter is Voice, best team leader is People, calm under pressure and perseverance are Inner Game, most creative is Build. The ladder formalises what the family already values.
- **The business.** Monetisation is parked as a Mogul privilege, and the stewardship milestone is the safeguard that makes that promise real. Mentors at a ratio of one to five or six are a staffing line the engagement model already budgets.

## Open design questions

1. How many stars per level: fixed counts, or all milestones required. Suggest: all milestones, with stars showing progress, so a level cannot be gamed by cherry picking one strand.
2. Age banding: whether younger kids get adapted milestones at the same level names, or a junior track. Emma at six finished the day; the ladder should have a floor that honours that without competing.
3. Time floors: whether a minimum of one cohort per level applies, to protect cohort integrity against sprinting.
4. Who verifies at scale: parent plus mentor works for family and pilot cohorts; a rubric card per milestone will be needed once strangers run tables.
5. Whether Mentor is mandatory to reach Mogul, or whether a parallel builder heavy route exists for kids who are brilliant but not ready to lead. Suggest: mandatory. The People strand is the point.
