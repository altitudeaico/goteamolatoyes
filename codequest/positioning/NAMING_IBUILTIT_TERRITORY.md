# The "I built it" naming territory
## Variations, and what is already taken

Written 12 August 2026, off the back of `ibuiltit`. Everything here sits in one territory: **the name is the sentence the child says**, in the first person and the past tense. That is a strong instinct. Past tense is proof. It names a thing that already happened rather than a promise about learning.

**Method and its limits.** Collisions below come from open web searching only. That surfaces live companies, obvious domains and some trademark filings, but it is not a registry search. Nothing here is legal clearance. Before committing to any name, run a proper UK IPO and USPTO search in the relevant classes (9 for software, 41 for education), and check the domains at a registrar.

## What the search actually found

The emotional territory is obvious enough that a lot of people have already landed on it. That is worth knowing before falling in love with any one option.

| Name | What is already there | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **ibuiltit** | `ibuiltit.co` architectural model kits, Bengaluru. `ibuiltit.co.uk` a Birmingham City University construction project. `ibuilt.net` modular construction, US. An IBUILTCOMPANY trademark. A YouTube channel. | Category is clear, since none of these are kids software. But every good domain is gone and the search results are noisy. Usable only with a different domain and a lot of SEO patience. |
| **imadeit** | **`imadeit.ai` is a live kids and teens business education company**, running camps where children build a product and present it. Also `imadeit.in`, a homemade goods marketplace. | Avoid. This is a direct collision in the same category and roughly the same audience. |
| **imadethis** | An X account, a Lemon8 handle, general craft use. | Weakly held, but the phrase is generic enough that it is hard to own and easy for others to use innocently. |
| **lookwhatimade** | An Etsy shop, a GitHub user, an X account. | Nothing serious, but it is long, awkward as a URL, and reads younger than fourteen. |
| **iMake** | The Faces iMake series, an established kids creative app family with awards. | Occupied in exactly our category. Avoid. |
| **kidsmade** | A 2024 US trademark filing, class 28, toy modelling dough. | Different class, so probably survivable, but it is a filing to note. |
| **makery** | A cooking franchise, a US ad agency, a Berlin DIY startup, a cannabis brand. | Too crowded to own. |
| **shipd** | A logistics company. | Taken, and "shipped" is developer language that adults love and children do not use. |

**The useful conclusion.** The plain-English versions of this sentence are largely picked over. What is still open is either a twist on the spelling, or the asset you already hold.

## The asset you already hold

**builtby.kids** is already bought. It is the strongest thing in this entire document and it is currently being treated as a URL rather than as a name.

The `.kids` top level domain makes the address itself finish the sentence:

```
elsie.builtby.kids      →  "Elsie, built by kids"
skybounce.builtby.kids  →  "Sky Bounce, built by kids"
```

The name, the domain and the proof are the same object. A child sends an address that says who made it. No explanation needed, in any country, at any reading age. It also happens to be exactly the deck's BUILT BY territory, which the strategist arrived at independently.

The known weakness, and the deck says this too, is that BUILT BY is hard to own as a company name because the words are common. Two ways round it:

1. **BuiltBy as the product and the publishing mark, with a different company name above it.** This is the deck's own recommended architecture, just with BuiltBy in the PLOT slot: the child publishes to BuiltBy, the company is called something else.
2. **Accept it as a product mark and stamp it on everything.** Every published project carries a Built By badge the way a book carries a publisher's colophon.

## Variations worth considering

Grouped by the mechanism each one uses.

### A. The sentence, first person, past tense

The `ibuiltit` instinct. Proof, not promise.

- **IBuiltThat** — the deck's own tagline as the name. Closer to natural speech than "it".
- **IShippedIt** — precise and adult. Children do not say shipped, which kills it for the primary audience.
- **WeBuiltIt** — the collective version. Fits team projects and events, loses the individual claim.
- **IPutItOnline** — describes the actual hero moment rather than the making. Long, but nobody owns it, and it is exactly what the product does.

### B. Twisted spelling, which is where ownability actually lives

Worth noting that Elsie got here first, on her own: **Butterflii**. She doubled a letter to make a common word ownable, which is the same instinct behind Flickr, Tumblr and Grindr. Following a nine year old's brand instinct is not a joke, it is a signal.

- **Bilt** — clean, but Bilt Rewards is a large US fintech. Crowded.
- **Byld** — likely open, but hard to say aloud and looks like a typo.
- **Bildit** or **Bilditt** — the Butterflii move applied to "build it". Probably ownable, phonetically obvious, and playful without being childish.
- **Buildr** — the 2010s startup suffix. Feels dated now.
- **Madeit** as one word — softer than the "i" prefix and avoids the imadeit.ai collision by dropping the pronoun, though it is close enough to invite confusion. Risky.

### C. Ownership and territory, the deck's PLOT direction

These name the place rather than the act, which solves a different problem: the child needs somewhere that is theirs.

- **Plot** — the deck's recommendation. Common word, so the trademark position depends entirely on class.
- **MyPatch** — warmer and more British than Plot. A patch is a small piece of ground you tend.
- **MyCorner** — gentle, unthreatening to parents, easy for a six year old.

### D. The publish moment itself

Nobody in the checked set is standing on the moment the thing goes live, which is the actual hero moment of the product.

- **ItsLive** or **It'sLive** — what a child shouts when it works.
- **NowItsLive**
- **WentLive** — past tense again, proof again.

## The three I would actually take forward

**1. BuiltBy, using builtby.kids.**
You own it. The address completes the sentence. It matches the deck's authorship territory and the emotional payoff of "I built that". Strongest as the publishing mark and the product name, with the company named separately if needed.

**2. Bilditt, or another twisted spelling of "build it".**
The most likely of anything here to be genuinely ownable across domain, trademark and app store. It carries the same sentence as ibuiltit without any of the collisions. It also follows the instinct one of your own kids already had.

**3. ItsLive.**
Names the moment rather than the making, which is the thing that actually makes a child's face change. Untested for collisions, so check it before getting attached.

**And one to rule out now: anything built on "I made it".** `imadeit.ai` is running kids' business education camps today. Same category, same audience, same words. That one is closed.

## What to do next

1. Registrar check on the shortlist, both `.com` and `.kids`, plus the obvious social handles.
2. UK IPO and USPTO searches in classes 9 and 41 for the two or three that survive step one.
3. Say each finalist out loud to a child and to a parent who has never heard of any of this. Ask them what they think it does. That test kills more names than lawyers do.
4. Whatever survives, try it on inside the app. The creator variant has a name switcher on its sign in screen for exactly this: a name should be judged wearing the product, not sitting on a slide.
