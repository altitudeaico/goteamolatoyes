# The Creator Ladder
## Variant B, written to Mayowa's positioning

Parallel to the original ladder. Nothing in the original is changed. This version tests what the pathway looks like if the identity is creator and publisher rather than coder, and if coding is one medium among several.

**What changed and why**

1. **The rungs are renamed to the act, not the rank.** Maker, Publisher, Guide, Owner. Each is a thing you have visibly done. Quester and Mogul were internal words that had to be taught; these do not.
2. **Publishing is the spine.** Every gate is crossed by putting something real into the world at its own address, not by finishing a lesson.
3. **The medium is open.** A game, an art thing, a story, a music thing, a little app. The Elsie test applies: if a girl who wants to be a makeup creator cannot see herself on this ladder, it has gone too coding-niche.
4. **Coding and AI become pillars, not the product.** They appear as capabilities that unlock a milestone, never as the milestone itself.
5. **The Build strand becomes Craft**, so it can hold visual, narrative and audio work as honestly as it holds code.

The four strands still run through every level, and the ratio still shifts from making toward voice and people as you climb. That was the strongest part of the original and it survives.

---

## The four strands, restated

**CRAFT.** Making the thing well, in whatever medium. Code, visuals, writing, sound, product sense. Understanding what you made well enough to change it on purpose.

**VOICE.** Naming, explaining, presenting, and eventually launching. The bit that turns a thing into a thing people know about.

**PEOPLE.** Working with others, receiving and giving feedback, resolving disagreement, and eventually leading and growing other makers.

**INNER GAME.** Finishing, recovering, coming back after the novelty dies, and eventually carrying wins and losses in public without either going to your head.

---

## MAKER
### "I made a thing and it is on the internet"
Ratio: roughly 70% Craft, 15% Voice, 10% People, 5% Inner Game.

**Craft**
- Make your first thing and get it live at its own address. Any medium.
- Change it on purpose three times, and be able to say what each change did.
- Use the AI helper to do something you could not do alone, then explain what it did in your own words. Authorship survives the help.

**Voice**
- Stand up and explain it for about 90 seconds: what it is, how to use it, one thing that went wrong.
- Name it. A real name that you chose.

**People**
- Give one specific compliment about someone else's thing. Specific means naming the part.
- Ask for help once.

**Inner game**
- Finish something in one day.
- The moment it broke and you carried on.

**GATE to Publisher:** it is live at its own address, you explained it out loud, and you sent the address to at least one person outside the room.

---

## PUBLISHER
### "I make things people actually use"
Ratio: roughly 50% Craft, 25% Voice, 15% People, 10% Inner Game.

**Craft**
- Publish three things. Each does something the last one could not.
- Work in a second medium at least once. A game maker writes something, an artist makes something interactive.
- Fix something out loud: make it happen again, find where it lives, change it.

**Voice**
- Write the words on your own page. That is your first piece of marketing and it is yours.
- Make a short video about something you made.
- Watch two real people use your thing without helping them, then change one thing because of what you saw. This is where product sense starts.

**People**
- Make one thing with somebody else, splitting the work and fitting the pieces together.
- Give structured feedback to another maker: I like, I wish, what if.

**Inner game**
- Publish something after the excitement wore off. The single most predictive milestone on the whole ladder.
- Keep one thing that did not work, named honestly, where you can see it.

**GATE to Guide:** three things live, one of them changed because of what a real person did with it, and you can tell the before and after story.

---

## GUIDE
### "I help other people make theirs"
Ratio: roughly 30% Craft, 25% Voice, 35% People, 10% Inner Game.

**Craft**
- Help someone else fix their thing without touching their keyboard. Questions first.
- Make something with a team in a shared space.
- Cut something on purpose so the thing could ship, and say what you cut and why.

**Voice**
- Teach one short lesson to younger makers.
- Introduce or host part of an event.
- Take questions on your feet after presenting.

**People**
- Guide a table of five or six through making and publishing something.
- Sort out one creative disagreement: each person says what they want, then together find the option neither had alone.
- Spot someone about to give up, and step in kindly.

**Inner game**
- Coach someone through a failure without fixing it for them.
- Set a public goal for your group and own the result out loud either way.

**GATE to Owner:** you guided a group, you taught at least once, and someone you guided got their own thing live.

---

## OWNER
### "I run something that is mine"
Ratio: roughly 20% Craft, 30% Voice, 35% People, 15% Inner Game.

**Craft and product**
- Run one thing properly over time: versions, a list of what changed, a list of what is next.
- Make a real trade off and write down why, where your team can see it.

**Voice and audience**
- Run a launch: a trailer, fresh words on the page, a plan for who hears about it.
- Read your numbers, visits and shares and returns, and say what you will try next because of them.
- Tell the story to adults: present to parents, partners or judges.

**People and leadership**
- Lead a small team through making and publishing one thing, everyone credited.
- Bring one new person in and get them to their first published thing.

**Inner game**
- Set the standard for your group. Take a loss publicly without blaming. Hand credit for wins to whoever did the work.
- The stewardship conversation: when earning becomes possible, sit with your parent and agree what it is for. Kids keep 100%. Owners learn what that means.

**GATE: The Awakening.** Granted on the whole record of what you made, published, explained, and who you helped. Verified by a guide and a parent together. Never bought.

## Head to head with the original

| | Original | Creator variant |
|---|---|---|
| Rungs | Quester, Builder, Mentor, Mogul | Maker, Publisher, Guide, Owner |
| First strand | Build, meaning tech and code | Craft, meaning any medium |
| What a gate needs | A shipped build | A published thing at its own address, any medium |
| Coding | The substance | One pillar among several |
| AI | A helper feature | A capability that must leave authorship intact |
| The Elsie test | She has to become a coder to climb | She climbs as an artist who publishes |
| Risk | Too narrow, competes in the crowded coding lane | Too broad, harder to say what you are buying |

**The honest tension.** Mayowa's warning is that coding is a red ocean and creator is the white space. The counter risk is that "creator platform for kids" is vaguer at the point of sale than "your kid builds real apps", and a parent buys the specific thing. One resolution worth testing: sell the specific, deliver the broad. The event and the first promise stay concrete, and the ladder above is what the child discovers once inside.

That is exactly the pattern Bolaji described in the debrief: be specific about what someone is buying, and let the breadth be the surprise after the first gate.
