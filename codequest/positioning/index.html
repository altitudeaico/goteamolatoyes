The test that decides it

If you cannot imagine an eleven year old girl who wants to be a makeup creator wanting to come to this thing and build something, it has gone too coding niche.

Mayowa, 12 August

Version B carries no name on purpose. If coding is only the medium, a name built on code describes the tool rather than the thing, so the variant lets you try the alternatives on and see which one the app can actually wear. Version A asks her to become a coder. Version B asks her to make something and gives her somewhere to put it, and coding is one of the ways she might do it. That is the whole argument, and it is worth deciding on its merits rather than on which screen looks nicer.

The counter argument deserves equal weight. A parent buys a specific thing. "Your kid builds real apps" is easier to pay for than "a creator platform for kids". One resolution worth testing: sell the specific and deliver the broad. Keep the concrete promise at the point of sale, and let the breadth be what they discover after the first gate.

What has no variant, and why

  • The story deck. Parked at your request. Nothing done to it either way until you have agreed the shape.
  • The 3D realm. Art direction, not positioning. If the creator route wins, the whole quest world is a bigger question than a variant can answer, and it would be a rebuild rather than a rewording.
  • The 2D simulation. Same reason. Its entire grammar is Herald, Forge, Guardian, Beacon. Under the creator positioning it does not get reworded, it gets replaced.
  • The portfolio and governance page. Positioning neutral. How the family holds equity does not change with the name.
  • The cutting room and the handoff. Internal tools. No audience, no positioning.
